How LiverCheck works

A student machine learning project using public survey data to explore non-invasive liver screening.

1. What it measures

The model looks at three main aspects of your health:

Liver cell stress

ALT, AST, and GGT. These enzymes leak into the blood when liver cells or bile ducts are under strain.

ALT · AST · GGT

Platelets and age

Platelets drop when a stiff liver causes blood to back up into the spleen.

Platelets · Age

Metabolic factors

BMI and waist size. Extra fat around the belly is closely linked to fat deposits in the liver.

BMI · Waist

2. How the model was trained

We used data from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). The training set comes from the 2017 to 2018 survey cycle (4,746 adults). Each person had routine blood work and a FibroScan exam, which measures liver stiffness in kilopascals (kPa). We set the target to 8.0 kPa or higher, which indicates significant scarring.

We calibrated the model with isotonic regression so that the predicted percentages match true observed rates.

3. Results on an independent test group

Tested on 5,526 separate adults from the 2021 to 2023 NHANES survey. The model was never adjusted on this group.

4. Limitations to keep in mind

US data only

The model was trained on US adults and may not perform the same way in other populations.

FibroScan estimates, not biopsy

FibroScan is a safe, painless scan, but only a tissue biopsy can prove scarring definitively.

Acute illness can distort numbers

Recent heavy drinking, a viral infection, or new medications can spike liver enzymes temporarily.

Student research only

This tool was built to learn and test machine learning concepts. It has not been reviewed by health authorities.

How to read your lab report

Where to locate ALT, AST, GGT, and Platelets.

ALT and AST appear on a standard metabolic panel (CMP) or liver function panel. In some labs, ALT is called SGPT, and AST is called SGOT. Platelets appear on a complete blood count (CBC).
